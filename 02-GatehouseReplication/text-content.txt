This project is a 1:1 scale replica of a Qing Dynasty gatehouse discovered during our fieldwork in a Beijing hutong. 
Over the course of four months, our six-member team built this physical reconstruction using standard stage design materials: a wooden framework, high-density foam, paper,and plaster.


In my attempt to fully reconstruct this weathered, 300-year-old Siheyuan wooden door, I dedicated a month of intensive nocturnal craftsmanship. Although the final physical replica achieved a high degree of visual realism for the audience, I know it still failed to capture even 80% of the artifact's true historical details.


To replicate the natural aging of the historical structure, I used a heated blade to melt foam into wood shape. I then applied plaster over the shaped surfaces to simulate the finer details of authentic wood grain.


While a high-resolution image captures the surface layout, it misses the tactile qualities that define the material's age. Subtle features—such as microscopic wormholes, the natural patina of old timber, and the texture of cracked lacquer layers—cannot be fully recorded through a lens. They require close visual and physical observation, which are essential for achieving true material authenticity.
