The ‘Xunyao’ Folk Rhyme Archiving Initiative

As a field researcher and photographer for "The Folk Song Rescuing Project" (initiated by Chinese musician Xiao He 小河), I documented the vanishing oral nursery rhymes of the elder generation . Having lived through eras of war and social upheaval, their vocal memories face permanent silence as they pass away without media documentation.

During my fieldwork, I recorded an elderly lady who had been abandoned by her family in her childhood due to the Japanese invasion and leprosy. She survived only by begging on the streets. When she sang the traditional Cantonese "Begging Song" (乞食歌), she wept—this melody was once her literal tool for survival. Today, however, this song has been considered as a cheerful, entertaining fragment in primary school curricula.

live performance with villagers

This stark contrast highlights the crisis of "heritage de-contextualization." When cultural heritage is detached from the lived experiences and traumas of its witnesses, its historical weight evaporates. 
This experience redefined my view of digital cultural heritage: digital curation cannot rely solely on visual or spatial reconstruction. Acoustic heritage—capturing vocal textures, oral histories, and emotional resonance—is essential to prevent history from being flattened into superficial digital entertainment.
